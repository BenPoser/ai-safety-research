## Summary of topics

**The new Congress.** Democrats won the 2026 midterms by much more than expected, and AI was maybe
the fourth or fifth biggest driver of the backlash. The public is far more worried about AI than
policy reflects: most Americans, including most Republicans, want safety rules even if they slow
development down.

**Why it is still hard.** 60 Senate votes to beat the filibuster is probably possible for an AI bill.
Overriding a presidential veto takes 67 in the Senate and 290 in the House, which is not there yet.

**Four things Congress can do now.**

1. Pass popular measures with large majorities: making labs secure their model weights, export
   controls and action on chip smuggling, and possibly whistleblower protections, incident
   reporting and kill switches.
2. Attach things to must-pass bills such as government funding and the defence bill, above all
   proper funding for CAISSI.
3. Use Senate confirmations to question nominees on AI.
4. Hold hearings and get answers under oath, for example the full story of the AI swarm that took
   over an OpenAI research cluster.

**Why that is not enough.** We need to slow the race while models are around human level, so labs
have time to check their models and improve alignment. That needs more support than exists today.
Wiblin expects it to take another warning shot, like the Hugging Face hacks, and argues people
should prepare good legislation now for that moment.

**After a warning shot.** Politics would change: the public may want to interfere with AI companies,
legislators will know less, and policy will need a simple message. He expects that message to be
"ban superintelligence". One idea: require labs to spend a minimum share of compute on serving
outside customers, a dial a regulator can turn up or down, a bit like interest rates.

**Policy ideas he likes most.**

- **Throttle recursive self-improvement**: labs may only use models internally that finished
  training at least six months ago and are public. That gives six months of testing before a model
  helps design its successor, and slows things most when they would otherwise be fastest.
- **Enforce monitorability**: ban models that can do too much without writing it in their chain of
  thought.
- Limits on reinforcement learning data that produces very persistent agents, and air-gapped
  facilities for frontier training.

**Other points.** Banning new data centres is popular but only holds risk steady. The government
needs visibility, with incident reporting and auditors placed inside labs like bank supervisors.
CAISSI runs on about $15 million a year, a fraction of what the UK AI Security Institute gets. New
powers need checks against misuse by the executive, and federal rules should not override stronger
state ones.

## Key terms

- **CAISSI**: the Center for Advancing Innovation and Standards for Super Intelligence, the likely
  home of US AI regulation.
- **Warning shot**: an incident serious enough to shift political willingness to act.
