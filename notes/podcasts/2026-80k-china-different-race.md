## Summary of topics

**The question.** The common argument against slowing down is that China will catch up. China's best
models are about six months behind America's, even though the US has roughly 16 times its AI
computing power. How?

**China's AI industry.** Tech giants (Alibaba, Tencent, ByteDance) and startups (DeepSeek, Zhipu).
Inventive and talented, and most open source their best models. But they are small and poor next to
the US labs, earning about 10% of what OpenAI and Anthropic do, and short of chips.

**Why China cannot make the best chips.** It cannot buy the most advanced chipmaking machines, which
only ASML in the Netherlands makes, and it cannot make enough of the fast memory AI chips need. The
US makes about 50 times as much AI computing power, largely because of export controls. Where they
are enforced, the controls work.

**Where China's chips come from.** Mostly from the US, in three ways:

- Bought legally, including gaming graphics cards that fall just under the export limits.
- Smuggled, such as top chips relabelled with a made-up brand.
- Rented in data centres abroad, the biggest channel. US rules stop sales to Chinese companies but
  not rentals, so they rent in Malaysia, Singapore and elsewhere.

**Copying models.** Chips alone would put China about 13 months behind. Distillation makes up much
of the rest: asking a stronger model millions of questions and training on its answers. Anthropic
says it cut what copiers can harvest by 90 to 99%, but copiers move to whichever company has the
weakest defences, and blocking them costs revenue.

**A different race.** China's own AI leaders say they are behind in the race for the smartest model.
Beijing seems to be running a different race: using AI everywhere. There it has real advantages in
electricity, factories and robots.

**Why a bigger lead matters.** Rodriguez dislikes the race framing, but a bigger gap gives breathing
room to test models properly and hold back unsafe ones. Matt Sheehan compares China to a wakesurfer
behind a boat: if the US slows, China has to rely on its own, smaller engine.

**Widening the gap.** Mostly with powers the government has: close the rental loophole, stop
approving chip sales, limit gaming cards and cut off older ASML machines and their maintenance. The
counter-argument is that controls push China to build its own chips sooner. Her answer is that
China was trying to anyway, and its own chips will not be ready in the next five years.

**Security.** Model weights are one large file, and the top US labs are rated around level 2 or 3 of
5 for security, where level 5 would stop the best state spy agencies. A stolen model gives away
dangerous capabilities without safeguards, and stolen techniques could close the gap for good.
Unlike chips, security is entirely in US hands.

**Conclusion.** If competing with China is the reason not to slow down, the first step is to stop
giving so much away.
