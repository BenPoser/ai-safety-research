## Summary of topics

**The report.** Anthropic has a line in its safety policy: the point where AI speeds up its research
enough to make two years of progress in one. Before reaching it, Anthropic says it will have much
stronger security and monitoring of its own AI development. In August, Anthropic said it had not
reached that line, and invited METR to check. METR spent 11 weeks inside the company, with access
to internal test scores, training data and staff interviews.

**The finding.** As of October 2026, Anthropic was improving its AI about twice as fast as a year
earlier, on tasks that are easy to measure. The range is 1.3 to 3 times. The main evidence is the
line of test scores across all of Anthropic's models, including three internal ones, which gets
about twice as steep around October 2025.

**Is AI causing the speed-up?** METR ruled out more compute as the cause, and hiring or new data
cannot explain why it has lasted. Its best guess is that about 80% of the speed-up comes from
Anthropic using its own AI in its research. The timing fits: it starts when Claude could code on
its own for hours. Anthropic's own numbers show Claude went from doing almost none of its research
work end to end early in the year to about a quarter by August, still with humans supervising. Most
of the evidence METR found convincing was redacted.

**No sign of slowing.** The loop only stops at a bottleneck: compute, data or ideas. METR found none
likely in the next year, and says the speed-up itself could be increasing. The episode compares
this with the AI 2027 scenario, where this kind of speed-up does not arrive until early 2027.

**Could METR be wrong?** The result held across many reanalyses. Toby Ord argues one model came from
a much bigger training run, and allowing for that one-off jump gives about 1.4 times. Buck
Shlegeris replies that the scores are just the part that was not redacted. The biggest caveat is
that it is all measured on easy-to-grade tasks. Ryan Greenblatt said the report proved him
seriously wrong and moved him towards superintelligence within one to two years.

**Has Anthropic crossed its line?** METR's run of Anthropic's private numbers gave 1.97. Anthropic's
IPO filing now says it has crossed its own line, and that it is evaluating the implications.

**What needs to change.** Companies will not stop alone because of competition, so government has
to act. The episode supports Alex Bores's call for Congress to ban AI companies from fully
automating their own research. It also points out the limits of outside review on the company's
terms: Anthropic decided what METR saw, tightened redaction in the final week, and METR worried that
being too critical would lose it access. At a minimum, every frontier company should report how
much AI speeds up its own research, with independent checks.

## Key terms

- **Recursive self-improvement (RSI)**: AI building the next, better AI, which then speeds up the
  next round, and so on.
- **METR**: an independent organisation that evaluates frontier AI risks.
